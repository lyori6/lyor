FindMePet: Using AI to Solve the Pet Adoption Mismatch Problem

Building an AI pet adoption app that matches people with pets that fit their lives, so fewer pets go back to shelters.

Lyor ItzhakiJanuary 1, 2025 · 2 min read
Problem
10 to 20% of adopted pets go back to shelters, often because of a poor match.
My role
Solo builder. Product, design, and code, with AI tools.
What I built
An adoption app that recommends pets based on a person's preferences and lifestyle.
Results
Shipped an MVP fast with a tight scope. Retired in December 2025.

The problem

Too many adoptions don't work out. Between 10 and 20% of adopted pets go back to shelters. Mismatched expectations and behavior issues are common reasons.

A University of Alberta study found 80% of adopters choose mostly on looks rather than fit. Meanwhile, 34% of pet owners buy from breeders, and 47% wrongly believe shelters only have older or poorly trained animals.

I adopted my dog Shasta and saw how hard the process is. I wanted fewer returns by making better matches from the start.

Key decisions

A focused MVP shipped fast beats a perfect product shipped late. So I made hard cuts:

  • Three animals, not dozens. The PetFinder API covers dogs to chinchillas. I launched with dogs, cats, and rabbits.
  • Filters upfront. Filtering happens in the API call, not on the results page. Simpler first version.
  • Photos first. Pets without photos rank lower, because photos drive the emotional connection.
  • Two paths. A guided questionnaire for people who want a recommendation. Quick Search for people who just want to browse.
  • One AI pick. Results show a grid with a single highlighted recommendation, to keep the choice easy.

How I built it

Next.js 14 on Vercel, the PetFinder API for live adoptable pets, and OpenAI (GPT-4o mini) for recommendations. PetFinder needed OAuth 2.0 through a Cloudflare Worker proxy. Getting structured, parseable recommendations took careful prompting.

I built it alone with v0, Cursor, Windsurf, and Claude. Getting to 70% was surprisingly easy. The last 20 to 30% (polish, edge cases, advanced features) took about 80% of the time. My best tip: have the AI document its work as it goes. You see what went wrong, and you can point it to the docs instead of re-explaining everything.

Results

The north star was successful adoptions. Early on, I tracked clicks on the Adopt button as the leading signal. Next on the list were richer lifestyle questions, a top three with comparisons, funnel analytics, and follow-ups to confirm real adoptions.

I retired FindMePet in December 2025, after the PetFinder API it depended on went away.

Takeaways

  • Tight scope ships. Three animal types and one AI pick got a working product out fast.
  • Set the north star before launch. Adoptions were the goal. Adopt clicks were the signal I could measure on day one.
  • PMs can build now. With AI tools, I went from idea to working app in a fraction of the usual time.
